Play is the retrieval leg of the loop, wearing different clothes

The stub reads as an arcade: four gallery cards in four unrelated palettes, four illustration styles, a filter for four items, and no statement of what any of them does to your vocabulary. An arcade is a second product. The version below is the same four games, re-sorted by the only question that matters here — does this drill my words — and each one says what it practises, what it costs, and what it needs before it can run.

What changed and why
The grid becomes a ranked list. Four equal tiles ask the user to choose between things they have never played. A list can put the one built from today's due words first and let the rest be a glance.
Sorted by “does this touch my words”. That is the honest axis, and it is the one Solo/PvP was standing in for. Higher or Lower sits under a rule and says plainly that a round changes nothing.
Every mode names its intent. Produce, recall, disambiguate — the same intents the domain model already carries, so a round of Crossword can post real review events instead of being a side activity.
The four palettes go. Teal, apricot, sand and cream are four hues off the ramp and four illustration styles. One quiet grid, drawn in plum on cream, for the primary only; the rest carry no art at all.
The filter goes. Three tabs over four items is chrome. Duel is the only two-player mode and says so in its own subtitle.
No card is clipped. Descriptions run to their end because the row grows; nothing here is a fixed-height tile with an overflow.
Unavailable is a state, not a lock. Duel has no partner, so it shows the reason and the action that fixes it. No greyed-out button, no padlock.
Cost stated before entry. “About six minutes, counts as review” is what decides whether someone starts. Play is competing with Review, so it should say when it substitutes for it.

Given games are parked, the cheap version of this is one page of markup: keep Crossword, hide the other three behind a link, and ship it the day the reader-to-card connection lands.
