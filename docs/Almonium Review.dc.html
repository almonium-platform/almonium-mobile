What this asserts, and why
A session is ten items, not the backlog. A queue that empties is finishable; a queue of 140 is a debt. Ten also makes the emblem an exact gauge — one blade per item — which is the only honest way to use it.
Intent is stated before the session, not inferred. “Six to understand, three to say, one to tell apart” is the differentiator in one line, and it explains why the same word can feel easy and still be due.
No self-graded buttons. Nothing on these screens asks the user to rate their own memory. The answer is typed, graded fuzzily and semantically, and the grade comes from what happened, not from a claim about it.
A wrong answer is a diagnosis, not a red flash. Naming the word the learner actually produced, side by side with the one that was asked, is the single most distinctive thing in the product and costs a lookup on a confusion edge.
Hints are priced, not hidden. Three rungs, each stated before it is taken, the last one being the source sentence. A hint taken is a recovered miss and is recorded as one — that is what keeps the schedule truthful.
“I just mistyped” exists. Without it, one fat finger writes a permanent confusion edge. With it, the correction is a first-class signal and the deck gets better than the model that seeded it.
Leeches leave the queue. Four failures in the same shape is evidence about the prompt, not the learner. They come back re-encountered — new sentence, different intent — and never as a card the user must lie to escape.
The dessert is the only warm surface here. Ink ground, light-end ramp, no mascot, no confetti: session complete is one of the three rationed celebrations, and it celebrates by giving the reader something to read.
The prompt wording rotates. Four to six paraphrases per sense are generated once at creation, so the fifth review does not train the shape of a sentence the learner has already memorised.
Provenance is on the card. “Saved 12 days ago while reading · seen 5 times” costs nothing and is the emotional half of the memory system. The full encounter history sits one tap behind the word.

What this needs that does not exist yet: FSRS on the backend rather than device-local intervals, ConfusionEdge with direction and count, the paraphrase pool, a hint ledger on ReviewEvent, and a leech rule. The dessert story is the only piece that spends tokens per use, which is why it is metered on free.

Decisions I made for you — overrule freely
Typing is the default answer mode for every intent, including “understand”, where a recognition tap would be faster and weaker. If that proves too heavy on mobile, the fallback is tap-to-choose for understand only, never for produce.
The session length is fixed at ten rather than user-set, because a settable number turns into a target and a target turns into a streak.
Confusion feedback appears on the wrong answer itself rather than at the end of the session. It costs the flow of the run, and it is worth it.
Pronunciation is missing from these screens entirely. It is an intent in the domain model and it needs TTS, which is the one workload that scales per user — so it is a premium surface and a separate draft.
