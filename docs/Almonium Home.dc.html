1c What goes on home, and what does not
In, and why
Continue reading, as the page header. Reading is the entrance to everything else, so it takes the eyebrow → serif headline → subhead → one action pattern and the page's only primary button. Position is a linear bar, never blades — prose is continuous.
Kept while reading. Four recent items with the sentence they came from and the chapter they came from. Encounter history is nearly free to build and it is the part of the product people describe to a friend.
Due today, as a count. One number, one secondary action, and a line naming the confusions in the queue. No gauge, because the reading bar is already the screen's one progress indicator.
Twelve-week record. Weeks, the user's own target, tint for time learning. No number can be lost, and there is no chain to break.
Discover in the navbar. Capture has to be one field away from anywhere. It is the same field a stranger uses on the landing page.
Plan, stated as a cap. "Free covers one language and a hundred saved words." Grey text, plum link, no bar, no raspberry. A paywall boundary is not an error.
Out, and why
Streaks, day counters, flame. The audience chose Almonium for not being gamified. Weeks are the unit and the target is theirs.
Almo, on the returning view. Home holds content, so it is a calm surface. He appears on 1b, where there is nothing to read, and nowhere else on this route.
Games, chat, friend activity. Games are parked until the loop retains anyone. Chat is transport, never a marketed surface. A feed of other people's progress is a different product.
A stats wall. Words known, accuracy, retention curves, level estimate. Four numbers on a hub screen is decoration; they belong on the profile, where someone went looking for them.
An upgrade card. The ask lives at the wall — the second language, the hundred-and-first word, the private import. One quiet line here is the whole of it.
A second primary button. Start review is outlined ink. One commit action per view, and today the commit is to read.
