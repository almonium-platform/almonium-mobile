Hover — what to do and what to skip
Do — darken one ramp step, lift 1px, deepen the shadow. 160ms, ease.
Do — a pressed state. Almost nobody builds one, and it is the single thing that makes a button feel physical.
Skip — scaling. Under 1.03 it is invisible; over it, the text resamples and looks soft.
Skip — dimming on hover. It says "less available" at the moment you want the opposite.
Skip — sweeps, glows, ripples, icon slides. That is where cheap comes from, and the sweep is already spoken for by premium.

What the two new steps do

#2C2530 replaces every use of black and of #3d3d3d body grey — it is a plum-tinted near-black, so headings and body text sit inside the family instead of beside it. #3A1462 is the rung between violet and ink: dark-button rest state, spine backs, footer, and the deep end of any gradient that needs more range than #5A1A74 gives.

Neither adds a hue. Both continue the same traverse the emblem already makes and simply carry it further down, which is why the ramp still cannot clash with itself.

2b Disabled buttons — opacity is the wrong tool
Current — opacity .6 on gradient
Continue

Still saturated, still gradient, still the most colourful thing on screen — so it still looks like the thing to press. And the label at .6 drops under contrast minimums, so the one part a user needs to read is the part that got weakest.

Proposed — flat, dropped out of the family
Continue

A neutral plum-tinted grey with full-strength text. It recedes because it left the brand family, not because it faded — and the label stays legible at 4.5:1. Losing the gradient is itself the signal, now that gradient means active premium.

Better still — say why
Continue
Pick a language first

A disabled button with no explanation is a dead end — the user can see the exit and not the lock. One line naming the unmet condition converts a blocked state into an instruction, and it costs nothing.

On cursor: not-allowed

Keep it — it is the honest cursor, and it is the only affordance blind to colour. But pair it with pointer-events: auto on a wrapper so the button can still receive hover and show a tooltip; a genuinely inert element cannot explain itself.

Never opacity on a container, either — it fades the shadow and any border along with the fill, so the button loses its edge and starts to dissolve into the panel. Set the disabled colours directly.

.btn-primary:disabled {
  background: #e8e2e6;
  color: #8b8290;
  box-shadow: none;
  cursor: not-allowed;
  opacity: 1;
}

The two inset hairlines are the whole trick: a pale line along the top edge and a dark one along the bottom make the disc read as a struck metal token catching room light. 1f Blade progress — discrete blades, not a water level
6 / 10
Session
10 / 10
Complete

Ten blades is ten units, so the mark is a natural discrete gauge — each blade snaps in with a 180ms bloom as you clear a card. Right for anything countable: a review session, onboarding, the words in a chapter.

And yes — static emblem, blades filling. Not the unfold. The unfold is a transition and reads as "something is happening"; a gauge has to be readable at rest, and an animating mark can't be. Keep them separate: unfold for load and discover, blades for progress.

Don't use it for reading progress on a book — 10% granularity is too coarse for 300 pages, and a novel is continuous rather than countable. Keep the linear bar there, and put the blades where the unit is a card.
