Is the style right
Yes. Keep this hand, fix three things.

It is the engraved plate the constitution asked for: hatching and stipple, no airbrush, no gloss, no rim light, no cartoon eyes, and it sits in the same family as the vintage engravings already in the games. The eyes carry warmth without being cute, which is the hard part and the reason this can go next to a paywall without the product reading as a beginner app. Lock it as the canonical figure.

Re-ink to #612B5E. The current line is a warm plum-brown, roughly #5A3A52. Next to the emblem it reads sepia, not plum. This is a recolour at the vector stage, not a re-render.
Too much detail to survive 40px. Sucker rows and stipple turn to grey mud below ~64px. The vector redraw needs a reduced variant — mantle, eyes, four readable arms — the same way the logo needs its 5-blade small size.
Perfectly symmetrical, dead-centre, straight at the viewer. Correct for a specimen plate, static as an illustration. Every other pose should break the axis: weight on three arms, head off-centre, one tip curling out of frame.

Empty state — yes, that is his home. Every empty surface in the product gets him. This is the single highest-value pose and the reason the character exists.
404 — reuse, don't draw. Same pose as the empty state, different copy. A missing page and an empty shelf are the same emotion: he looked, there was nothing. Offline and 5xx are different — those are our fault, so they get the apologetic pose.
Celebrating — one pose, not three. Book finished, review session cleared, week's bar met all share a single quiet-approval pose. Three grades of celebration would be the gamification the audience picked you for avoiding.
Upgrade — before, not after. The paywall gets the offering pose: he holds out a card. The post-purchase screen gets nothing; confetti already fires once and a mascot taking a bow after you pay reads as a sales tactic.
Documented — the rules yes, the doctrine no. Constitution §7 has style, set, placement, naming; the extraction audit repeats it. Neither says what he is for. Paste-ready paragraph below.
Cost — two sheet renders. All seven poses come out of one character-sheet image, cropped apart. Budget a second pass for the cells that fail. Not seven separate generations — that is how you get seven different octopuses.

The doctrine that was missing

Almo's job is goodwill on the surfaces where the product has nothing to give. An empty shelf, a search with no result, a page that does not exist, a request that failed, a session with nothing due — these are the moments a user decides whether the product is thin or simply new. He absorbs them. He costs nothing when things work and he buys patience when they do not.

Which means the inverse is law: he never appears on a surface that is working. No mascot beside real content, no mascot in the reader, no mascot in the landing hero, no mascot taking credit for the user's progress. He shows up where the product is silent, and he leaves the moment it has something to say. One exception, earned: onboarding's last screen, where the greeting converts “setup finished” into “let's begin”.

The register is deadpan competence, not enthusiasm. He is the reason a dead end feels like a place, not a bug.

↳ Annotation, not UI copy — do not render. Rest pose standing in for searching. 120px, top-left, above the eyebrow, 14px clear space below him. Never centred, never larger than the heading's optical weight.
Size. 96–140px in a card, 160–200px on a full-page 404 or offline screen. One instance per screen, ever.
Placement. Left-aligned with the text block, above the eyebrow. He is the first thing read, so he sets the tone before the sentence explains it.
No plate, no disc, no tinted panel behind him. He is line art on the card surface. A container makes him an illustration slot; without one he is part of the page.
Motion. Fade and 6px rise on mount, 240ms, once. No idle loop, no float, no bobbing arms.
Dark mode. Second ink at the vector stage — the dark rulebook's engraving plum, not a filter, not an inversion. No new generations.

02Greetingtier 1

Onboarding's last screen, the one place he is introduced. Arms fanned behind him so the silhouette echoes the emblem's bloom.

03Tangledtier 1

Offline, 5xx, failed import. Apologetic, knotted in his own arms. The pose that does the actual goodwill work, because it appears when the user is annoyed.

04Approvaltier 1

Session cleared, book finished, week's bar met. Quiet, seated, a single slow nod of a gesture. No confetti, no arms in the air — delight, one notch below where you'd draw it.

05Offeringtier 1

Paywall and limit walls. One arm extends a blank card toward the viewer. Patient, not pleading — the difference is in the eyes staying level.

06Asleeptier 2

Nothing due, caught up, deliberate rest day. Says “this is fine” where a streak app would say “you're slipping”. Strategically the most on-brand pose in the set.

07Readingtier 2

Empty library, email headers, press and store assets. Never inside the reader itself. The one pose that also works as a brand image outside the product.

A · the sheet — one render, seven poses
Reference image attached: use this exact octopus character, unchanged in
anatomy, proportion, line weight and hatching density.

A single character sheet on a fully transparent background: the SAME
octopus character drawn seven times, arranged 4 across and 2 down, with
generous empty margin around each figure so the cells can be cropped
apart. No labels, no captions, no numbers, no borders, no grid lines,
no ground, no shadows, no scenery.

Style, identical in every cell: 19th-century engraved natural-history
plate. Hand-hatched line work and fine stipple, single deep plum ink
(#612B5E), no other colour, no airbrush, no gloss, no rim light, no
gradient shading, no outline glow. Full body, drawn large and clean
enough to read at 96px tall.

Character, identical in every cell: rounded stippled mantle, two large
calm forward-facing eyes with a soft brow fold, eight tapering arms
with double rows of suckers and curling tips. Same individual animal
every time. No hat, no glasses, no clothing, no props other than the
ones named, no speech bubbles, no anthropomorphic hands or feet.

The seven poses:
1. SEARCHING — leaning forward off its own axis, one arm lifting the
   edge of a flat closed book to peer underneath, another arm shading
   its eyes, head tilted, curious not worried.
2. GREETING — upright, arms fanned symmetrically out and behind the
   body like an opening fan or a flower in bloom, one front arm raised
   in a small wave, eyes bright and level.
3. TANGLED — arms loosely knotted around itself in one soft overhand
   knot, one arm holding the frayed end of a cut cord, mantle slumped
   slightly, sheepish apologetic expression.
4. APPROVAL — settled and low, weight on three arms, a closed book
   tucked under one arm, one arm raised in a single small unhurried
   gesture of approval, eyes half-lidded and content. Restrained.
5. OFFERING — one arm extended forward toward the viewer holding a
   single blank rectangular index card upright, other arms at rest,
   gaze level and patient.
6. ASLEEP — curled compactly, mantle drooping to one side, arms coiled
   loosely beneath the body, eyes closed as two soft curved lines. No
   pillow, no bed, no z's.
7. READING — head bent over an open book held by two arms, a third arm
   tracing a line across the page, other arm tips curled inward.

Expect the hex to be ignored. No image model holds a single flat spot colour reliably. Generate in whatever near-plum it gives you, then flatten to #612B5E in one pass at the vector or PNG stage. Judge the renders on pose and line quality only.
Arm count fails often. Count to eight in every cell before accepting it. Seven arms and nine arms both happen, and on a mascot whose whole premise is eight arms it is the one error nobody will forgive.
Then get it redrawn. These renders are the brief for an illustrator, not the shipping asset. Seven vector poses, one ink variable, two inks for light and dark, a reduced variant for ≤64px. That is the deliverable the constitution's “commission once” is pointing at.
