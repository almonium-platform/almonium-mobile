Almonium · Almo

Seven poses, fifteen slots, one job

The set is closed. Six poses come from a single character-sheet render; rest is the original figure. What remains is vector work, not drawing work.

Doctrine
He owns the moments the product has nothing to give

Almo's job is goodwill on the surfaces where the product has nothing to give. An empty shelf, a search with no result, a page that does not exist, a request that failed, a session with nothing due — these are the moments a user decides whether the product is thin or simply new. He absorbs them. He costs nothing when things work and he buys patience when they do not.

Which means the inverse is law: he never appears on a surface that is working. No mascot beside real content, no mascot in the reader, no mascot in the landing hero, no mascot taking credit for the user's progress. He shows up where the product is silent, and he leaves the moment it has something to say. One exception, earned: onboarding's last screen, where the greeting converts “setup finished” into “let's begin”.

The register is deadpan competence, not enthusiasm. He is the reason a dead end feels like a place, not a bug.

The set
Seven drawings, final
Rest3 slots

Onboarding's last screen, empty social, not enough words to play. Greeting and waiting are contexts, not poses — this drawing covers both.

Searching4 slots

Empty shelf, no search result, no cards kept, 404. The pose that pays for the whole set. The book clears the arms on both sides, which is why it survives being small.

Tangled1 slot

Offline, 5xx, failed import. The pose doing the real goodwill work, because it arrives when the user is annoyed. Full-page only — the knot needs 160px to read.

Approval3 slots

Session cleared, book finished, week's target met. One pose for all three: three grades of celebration would be the gamification the audience picked you for avoiding. Nothing held, nothing raised.

Offering1 slot

Limit walls only. Card held up and clear of the body, engraved border, nothing printed on it. An offer you can decline.

Asleep1 slot

Nothing due, caught up, a deliberate rest day. Says “this is fine” where a streak app says “you're slipping”. The most on-brand pose in the set and the most legible at any size.

Reading2 slots

Empty library, email headers, press and store assets. Never inside the reader itself. The one pose that also works as a brand image outside the product.

Not in the set

Greeting and waiting are rest. Loading is a skeleton's job. Thinking, writing, and anything holding a prop other than a book or a card: drawings without slots.

Also not his: account deleted, auth screens, post-checkout, the pricing table, and every surface holding real content.

What's left
Five vector-stage fixes, then it ships

Don't generate again. The set has consistent anatomy, line weight and stipple because it came out of one render; every remaining fault is something a vector pass fixes for certain and a new render would risk.

1. Knock out the ground. The sheet came back on white. Empty-state cards are #F9F6F5, so a white square would show.
2. Flatten the ink to #612B5E. Closer than earlier runs but still a shade warm. No image model holds a flat spot colour; do it in one pass afterwards.
3. Wake the eyes up. Most cells drifted half-lidded, which reads as one mood applied to six situations. Only asleep is closed, only approval is lidded; searching, offering and reading are alert. Cheap, and it is most of the character.
4. Un-flatten the arm on the page. In searching and reading the arm resting on the open book spreads into a webbed mitten. Restore the round cross-section and carry the sucker rows to the tip.
5. Vectorise, then add the variants. One ink variable; a second ink for dark mode (the dark rulebook's engraving plum, not an inversion or a filter); a reduced variant — mantle, eyes, four readable arms — for anything at or below 64px. Count to eight in every pose before locking.
Filenames. almo-rest, -searching, -tangled, -approval, -offering, -asleep, -reading. Pose name, no version numbers — a v2 in a filename means the set drifted.

↳ Annotation, not UI copy — do not render. Limit wall, the one exception. Offering at 88px in a left column, top-aligned, 18px gutter — a sheet has no vertical room for the default treatment.
Size. 96–140px in a card, 160–200px on a full-page 404 or offline screen, 88px in a sheet. Nothing below 64px without the reduced variant. One instance per screen, ever.
Placement. Left-aligned with the text block, above the eyebrow. He is read first, so he sets the tone before the sentence explains it. Never centred.
No plate, no disc, no tinted panel behind him. Line art directly on the card. A container makes him an illustration slot; without one he is part of the page.
Motion. Fade and 6px rise on mount, 240ms, once. No idle loop, no float, no bobbing arms.
Props cross the silhouette. Below roughly 96px the body is a solid stippled mass, and anything held in front of it merges and stops existing. A prop clears the outer edge or it is deleted.
Naming by exposure. Name him in passing in copy. Never a nameplate, never a “meet Almo” moment.
For later additions only
The prompt that produced this set

Kept for the day a genuinely new slot appears. Attach the approved rest figure and the reading pose as references — never the newest output. Write the pose as body mechanics; naming an emotion is what makes the model reach for human anatomy, which is where thumbs and furrowed brows came from.

Reference images attached: use this exact octopus character, unchanged
in anatomy, proportion, line weight, mantle stipple and hatching
density. Where a book appears, draw it exactly like the book in the
attached reading pose.

One figure, centred, on a fully transparent background. 19th-century
engraved natural-history plate: hand-hatched line work and fine
stipple, single deep plum ink (#612B5E), no second colour, no airbrush,
no gloss highlight, no rim light, no ground, no shadow, no scenery, no
text, no frame. Drawn large and clean enough to read at 96px tall.

ANATOMY — strict:
- Eight arms. Count them. Each tapers to a smooth pointed tip and
  carries its double rows of suckers all the way to the tip.
- NO thumbs, NO fingers, NO knuckles, NO nails, NO palms, no hands or
  feet, nothing anthropomorphic. An arm tip is a taper and a curl,
  never a grip made of digits. An arm resting on a surface keeps its
  round cross-section — never a paddle, flipper or mitten.
- The face contains only: mantle, one soft fold above each eye, two
  large calm forward-facing eyes. NO eyebrows, no creased or furrowed
  forehead, no wrinkles, no lips, no human nose, no aged human face.
- No hat, no cap, no glasses, no clothing, no speech bubble.
- Props: only an open book (visible spine, two splayed pages, hatched
  page block) or a single upright rectangular card with a thin engraved
  border. No cords, tassels, ribbons, bookmarks, scrolls or boxes. Any
  prop is held out clear of the body so it crosses the outer edge of
  the silhouette.

POSE, as body mechanics only — which arms bear weight, where the head
tilts, how far the eyes are lidded. Do not name an emotion:
<write the pose here>

Break the symmetry of the reference: weight on three arms, head off the
centre axis, one arm tip curling out of frame.
A new pose needs a slot that reuses it. Three of the seven serve three or four surfaces each; that ratio is what keeps the character from becoming decoration. If a proposed pose serves one screen and does not replace anything, the answer is copy, not a drawing.
