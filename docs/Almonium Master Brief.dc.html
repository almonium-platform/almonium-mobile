Almonium: the whole brief

Everything an agent, a collaborator, or a future you needs before touching the product: what it is, who it is for, what is decided, what is differentiated, how it makes money, what to build next, and the rules the interface obeys. Nothing here is aspiration — where the product does not yet do something, it says so.

1 · What it is

A language-learning system covering the full acquisition lifecycle of a word, phrase, or chunk: encounter it, understand it in context, decide whether it is worth learning, save it with provenance, retrieve it through varied practice, diagnose exactly how the knowledge is failing, and rehearse it until it becomes usable language.

Reading, Discover, review, stories, sharing, and games are surfaces around one personal knowledge and memory system. They are not separate products.

Working proposition
Read anything. Keep every word.

It names both surfaces, implies the loop, and takes nothing Readlang already owns. Every competitor owns a verb — read, input, listen, drill, mine. The unclaimed verb is what happens to a word after you meet it. “Multitool” is the worst available position and is retired.

2 · Who it is for, and who built it

User: self-directed intermediate-to-advanced learners who already consume real content and already collect vocabulary across several tools. Often multilingual. Not beginners, not casual gamified learners. They chose Almonium for not being gamified, and nothing may read as a beginner app.

Wedge under consideration: Ukrainian and Russian speakers learning German. Large, motivated, deadline-driven — integration courses, B1 for residency, professional recognition — and completely underserved by tools built for English speakers. The founder has native access to those communities.

Founder: solo, SWE, Kyiv. Took his own English to C2 self-taught, now learning German, speaks four languages. Started as a NaUKMA BSc project in 2022; the core thesis has been stable since. Founder-problem fit is genuine. Marketing and distribution are the weak side, and he knows it.

3 · Current state

Real, substantial, and not yet closing its own loop.

Built

Spring/Java backend, Angular web, React Native mobile in progress, self-hosted Postgres, Paddle Billing, Stream Chat, Firebase, custom auth, a Chrome extension, a Python book processor run manually, parallel reader, cards with device-local review, social graph, four game shells.

Missing

The reader-to-card connection, backend-synced scheduling, a landing page, honest terms and pricing, and any users who are not friends.

Infrastructure: Oracle pay-as-you-go, self-hosted DB, Backblaze B2 backups, Zepto Mail. Near-zero monthly cost.

Known failure mode

The founder builds whatever feature is interesting when he reaches it, and the digressions consistently land on invisible work with no audience — custom auth, avatar pickers, settings, chat — at exactly the moments when the next honest step was showing the product to a stranger. Four years of stable vision, zero external users. The constraint is not capability or capital. It is that progress has meant “features exist” rather than “strangers came back.”

4 · What is genuinely differentiated

Ranked by defensibility.

  1. Confusion-aware feedback. Answer A with the meaning of B, and Almonium names B and contrasts them. No competitor found doing this. Deterministic, cheap, shippable in a week. Highest edge per hour of work in the product.
  2. Receptive vs productive intents. “I understand it but cannot say it” as a first-class state with its own exercises. Everyone else has one number. Deepest idea, hardest to put on a landing page.
  3. Level-adapted real books. The same novel at B1, B2, C1. Beelinguapp grades short original content; nobody adapts real literature. $1–5 per book per level. The one an ordinary learner understands in a sentence.
  4. Multi-fluent-language triangulation. Meaning shown across every language the learner speaks, because Ukrainian sometimes catches what English flattens. Novel, narrow audience, near-zero cost.
  5. Paraphrase rotation. Reword the answer each review so the learner stops memorising the shape of the card.
  6. Encounter history. Where a word was first met, how often, what it was confused with. Emotional differentiator, nearly free.
  7. Dessert story. A short generated passage at the end of a session containing the words just failed. Best session-ending hook available.
Not differentiated — do not claim otherwise

Parallel reading (Beelinguapp has 4M+ downloads and it is their whole method), click-to-translate reading (Readlang, LingQ, Migaku), frequency ordering (Migaku, Clozemaster), deck sharing (Anki, Quizlet), browser extensions (Readlang, Language Reactor, Toucan), diglot-weave blending (Prismatext). Beelinguapp is parallel reading as the destination; Almonium is parallel reading as the entrance to a memory system. Say that, and never claim to have invented the format.

What they do better: language coverage (Readlang claims 100+, Almonium has two it can do well), content volume, distribution (Steve Kaufmann's million subscribers is worth more than the whole backlog), time in market, and the fact that they have shipped. Distribution is the whole game.

6 · Decisions made — do not relitigate

Question Decision
Angular → React rewriteNo. Extract a shared TS API client and domain types instead (~1 week). Revisit only if Angular becomes a measured velocity blocker.
Per-language DB tablesNo. 2–5M lexeme rows is ordinary Postgres. Partition review_events by month only.
Prefill the whole lexiconNo. Write-through cache + 10k-lemma warm-up + book-driven enrichment.
Oxford / WordsAPI / TwinwordNo. An LLM produces a richer structured entry for a fraction of a cent and you own it.
Frontier model at runtimeNo. Nano/flash for cached generation, mid-tier for translation and adaptation, frontier only for designing prompts.
Launch at $5, raise laterNo. Launch at target price with a generous free tier; grandfather founding members.
Chat as a marketed featureNo. Transport for shared learning items and study-buddy pairs. Never on the landing page.
DeepL for book translationNo. ~5–30× more expensive per novel and worse at literary register.
Harness vocabulary and target valuesThe word is learning, never reading — “time learning”, “filling in as you learn”, with “minutes” used only beside a number — so that adding Play needs no copy change and a review-only week reads as a met week. Targets are once / twice / four times a week, or none. No daily option: a weekly bar of seven fails on any single missed day, which is a streak wearing a different hat. The target is edited only on home, in place, and Settings has no picker.
Surplus languages on downgradeDeactivated, at period end. Leaving them active makes one Premium month a permanent multi-language unlock. The user picks which language stays; the rest go read-only and keep every word and review, deletion never enters it. Afterwards the active language can be changed once a month, and the downgrade pick does not spend that switch. Re-upgrade reactivates the previously active learners up to the new cap, most recent first. MAX_TARGET_LANGS has to be enforced on plan change, not only on add, and editable must mean active and within cap.
Requested alignments for library booksYes, as a Premium allowance: 3 requests a month, sitting beside the 3 custom imports. Free tier gets 1, because the output is amortised — unlike an import it serves every reader of that pair afterwards and becomes a public parallel-text page. Library books only, requested from inside a book you have opened, deduped per book and language, counts internal. Premium read first; no ETA and no queue position ever.
AI-generated book coversNo. Typographic covers. This audience is hostile to AI art and it undercuts the literary positioning.
Games, PvP, streaksParked until the core loop retains anyone. Instead: the harness — a self-set weekly target where any completed learning event (review, reading, play) counts toward a day, not configurable beyond the target itself.
Governing engineering principle

Pay a model at write time, serve deterministically at read time. Cache every reusable artifact with its model and prompt version. Never call a model on a path a user waits for unless the result is new. Cache warming rides on content processing: every book ingested enqueues enrichment for its vocabulary, so the library builds the lexicon.

7 · The domain model

LearningItem replaces Card as the domain object; a card becomes one presentation of an item.

LearningItem
  language, written form, normalized form, lemma
  type: word | phrase | chunk | template
  part of speech, selected sense
  learning intent: understand | produce | pronounce | disambiguate | chunk
  translations (multiple fluent languages)
  source contexts + encounter history
  frequency evidence with provenance
  confusion edges
  memory state (FSRS, server-side)

Supporting tables: Sense (one spelling, many senses, different CEFR levels), Encounter, ReviewEvent (append-only, month-partitioned; a power user generates ~70k rows a year), ConfusionEdge (confused_with, false_friend, sounds_like, near_synonym).

9 · Freemium

The rule that decides everything: free where cost is amortised, paid where cost scales per user. A processed book or a cached lexical entry costs the same for ten readers or ten thousand — free. TTS, custom import and per-use generation cost every time — metered and paid.

Free
A real reading tool

Unlimited reading, unlimited lookup, unlimited review sessions, confusion detection and paraphrase rotation included. 100 saved items, 1 target and 1 fluent language, one adapted chapter as preview, 3 dessert stories a week, 1 translation request a month. No audio, no import, no sync. Receives shared packs, cannot create them.

Premium · $12/mo, $120/yr
Everything, unlimited

Unlimited items and languages, all level-adapted editions, cross-device sync, unlimited stories under fair use, 10 hours of book audio, 3 custom imports a month, 3 translation requests a month read first, pack creation, encounter history and statistics.

Founding · $8/mo, $80/yr
First fifty, locked

Same as Premium. Say the number publicly and count it down honestly. A distinct Paddle price granting the same entitlement; the app reserves one of fifty durable slots before checkout and confirms from the webhook. Keep those records permanently.

The 100-item cap is the load-bearing limit. Someone who has saved a hundred words from a book has already had the experience and built something they do not want to lose. Two or three serious sessions reach it — long enough to see value, short enough to matter. LingQ's most common free-tier complaint is that the cap bites before users understand the system; do not repeat that.

Never gate reading, lookup, or confusion feedback. Reading is the SEO engine, lookup is cached and free to serve, and confusion detection is what people tell their friends about. Always gate audio, custom import, and anything generating fresh tokens per user per use.

Translation requests are a metered Premium perk with a free taste. Aligning a library book into a fluent language it lacks costs ~$2 once, so the allowance is 3 a month on Premium and 1 on Free — the free grant exists because, unlike a custom import, the result is amortised: it serves every reader of that pair afterwards and ships as a public parallel-text page. Spam control is intent plus the counter: the affordance exists only inside a book the account has opened, library titles only, requests dedupe by book and language so a second asker joins the first rather than spending again, and counts stay internal — at this user volume a visible vote tally reads as an empty room. Premium requests are read first. Never publish a date or a queue position; fulfilment is curated, gated by a monthly spend ceiling in /ops, and each approval lands in the external-cost ledger.

Finite, not annoying. Ads, delays, lockouts and nagging modals make people leave and say bad things. A hundred words reached, with nothing degraded, is a natural boundary. Every limit should read as the end of a generous allocation rather than a punishment.

No trial. A generous free tier already does the job, indefinitely. Trials add webhook complexity, abuse handling, and a day-fifteen churn spike that makes early retention unreadable. Revisit once a paid funnel works.

Regional pricing at 40–60% of list for UA, CEE, LATAM, SEA via Paddle overrides. Note Ukrainians living in Germany bill in the EU band, so the wedge splits across both.

Enforcement needs three things before launch: a limits table rather than scattered if premium checks; the per-user, per-feature, per-model cost ledger; and a real soft ceiling above any “fair use” number, handled as a conversation rather than a silent cutoff. Internal and QA accounts use an audited access grant, never a fake subscription. The pricing page must describe only limits the backend enforces.

Never charge for skins, avatars or cosmetics; streak repairs (Readlang sells these — it is a small revenue line and a large statement about what a product values); or the removal of friction you introduced in order to sell its removal.

10 · What to build next

Work top to bottom. Do not start a P1 while a P0 is open. Effort: S under a day, M two to five days, L one to three weeks.

P0 — nothing matters until these are done
Landing page (M) — today the login screen is the landing page and it converts nobody.
Public no-signup Discover demo (M) — a stranger pastes a German sentence and gets the full word sheet. The conversion mechanic.
Product analytics, PostHog (S) — signup, first lookup, first save, first completed session, D1/D7/D30, book opened, progress %, share created/opened, subscribe, cancel, platform on every event.
External-cost ledger (M) — impossible to retrofit, trivial now.
Terms and privacy that are not placeholders (S) — payments, chat, analytics, personal data. Real liability.
Align the pricing page with what the backend enforces (S) — it currently promises five things that do not exist.
Purge leaked credentials from the book processor (S), and verify the B2 restore path (S) — backups never restored are not backups.
P1 — the core loop, end to end

encounter → understand → save → review → varied retrieval → diagnose → re-encounter

Domain: introduce LearningItem alongside Card (L); Sense, Encounter, partitioned ReviewEvent, ConfusionEdge; a paraphrase pool of 4–6 wordings per sense, generated once at creation.
Review: scheduling to the backend on FSRS (L) — kill device-local intervals, do not invent your own; rotate the prompt form every review; fuzzy and semantic answer acceptance; confusion-aware wrong-answer feedback (M, the single most distinctive shippable feature); progressive weighted hints; leech handling that never makes a user press “remembered” to escape a card; the dessert story.
Capture: one-tap save with sensible defaults; reader selection opens the same Discover sheet without losing position; optional intent selector; always capture the source sentence.
Discover pipeline: LLM-generated structured entry, write-through cached with model and prompt version (L); warm the top 8–10k DE and EN lemmas offline via batch (M, single-digit dollars); enqueue enrichment from book ingestion; a user correction flow, which is how the DB gets better than the model that seeded it; wordfreq for frequency, with provenance fields.
NLP: extract into a Python FastAPI service (L), killing the 500MB Stanford bundle in the JVM; phrase-template matcher built once by AI, matched deterministically at runtime.
P2 — acquisition and content
Server-rendered public parallel-text pages (L) — an Angular SPA will not rank. One page per book, one per chapter, full text, no login wall. The only acquisition channel that scales without you. Plus sitemap, canonical URLs, schema.org Book markup, and auto-generated “N most useful words in Book” pages.
Shareable word packs with public preview; study-buddy pairing on the existing chat (density of two, not thousands).
Pipeline: Standard Ebooks as source where available, per-book YAML recipes, QA gates that fail loudly, canonical alignment groups (N editions not N²), embeddings + dynamic programming for sentence alignment with the LLM only on low-confidence spans, AI translation of PD originals into modern DE/UA labelled honestly.
Push notifications for due reviews — the biggest retention lever in SRS apps and the thing Readlang cannot do. Extension highlighting of learning items on any page. PPP price overrides. Fair-use limits in user-visible units. Credit-gated custom import, client-side EPUB parsing, private-only, with a takedown process.

Lab — only with a stated metric: in-page word substitution (great demo, weak pedagogy: it drills L1 syntax), rare-word poker, graffiti handwriting, homophone exploration, PvP anything, any additional dictionary API, Elasticsearch or Kafka.

11 · The design constitution, condensed

Full rules live in the design constitution document. These are the laws everything else derives from; if a rule and a law conflict, the law wins.

  1. No brand hue exists that is not on the emblem's ramp. Status colours are the only exception.
  2. Gradient means premium and nothing else. Every other action is solid.
  3. Calm by default, warm at the edges. Surfaces holding content are quiet; personality lives only where there is nothing to read.
  4. Two type families, self-hosted. Literata reads, IBM Plex Sans operates. No system stacks, no third family.
  5. Every panel is a card. Same radius, same shadow, white on cream.

Buttons: one primary per view, fully rounded, 600 weight. Primary solid #5A1A74; secondary outlines toward ink, never toward plum; premium is the only gradient, defined by two inset hairlines reading as struck metal. Disable for danger, never for incompleteness — an incomplete form keeps a live button that marks the offending field, because a disabled button cannot explain what is missing.

Register: calm on content surfaces (reader, library, cards, review, settings, landing hero), warm on contentless ones (empty states, errors, 404, offline, auth, onboarding, earned celebration). Almo the octopus and any particles live only in the second. Celebration is rationed to three events: first upgrade, book finished, session complete. Voice is second person, plain, unhurried; no exclamation marks, no emoji, no “awesome”, no streak threats. Progress is a record, never a threat: weeks are the unit, the user sets the target, “no target” is a first-class choice, and no number can be lost.

Surfaces: the card is the atom — white on cream, 24–28px on panels, 12–18px nested, no inner shadows. Flex or grid with gap for every sibling group. Blades are a gauge for countable units only; reading position is always a linear bar. Never two progress indicators on one screen.

Accessibility: 4.5:1 on body text, 3:1 on large text and boundaries; status never by colour alone; 44px touch targets; visible focus rings; all ambient motion behind prefers-reduced-motion. Status must never look like action, and an action must never be labelled with a state.

12 · Landing page rules

Server-rendered or statically generated. Five seconds to comprehension: one sentence, one moving image, one button. Show the real product — screen recordings beat illustrations, and every section gets one visual of the actual thing. One idea per section; one call to action repeated four times down the page. Specificity beats adjectives: “forty-two books in six languages, aligned paragraph by paragraph” beats “an extensive library”, and never write seamless, powerful, or revolutionary. Lead with what is rare and true, not with what took longest to build. Do not hide the price. Answer each objection where it arises. Medium length, eight or nine sections — the failure mode is filler, not length. Never claim to have invented parallel reading.

Order: hero → live paste demo → the bookshelf → understand any word → confusion-aware review (the wow section, most space) → the dessert story → send words to a friend → honest difference list → founder story → pricing → final call to action. The founder story replaces testimonials, because it is the only thing on the page a competitor cannot copy.

Leave off: chat, games, the tech stack, streaks and heatmaps, a competing newsletter signup, “trusted by” logos you do not have, and any adjective doing the work a screenshot should do.

Build order: hero plus final CTA today, even with nothing below it; then the bookshelf with real links to public reader pages; then the word sheet and the confusion section; then founder and pricing; then the live paste demo, which is engineering work rather than page work.
